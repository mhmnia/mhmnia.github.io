Volcanoes

Where to start if you want to understand what volcanoes do and how we watch them. These are the courses, reports and data services I keep coming back to, and the ones I send students to first.

Courses and lecture series

Structured teaching, from full university courses to recorded lecture series. All free to watch or audit.

edX

Volcanology — University of Iceland

learning.edx.org · UIcelandX UI02

A university course from a country that lives with volcanoes. Covers eruption styles, monitoring and hazard management with Icelandic case studies throughout, which means real crisis-response examples rather than textbook abstractions.

LEC

Volcanology lecture series

youtube.com · playlist

A recorded lecture course working through magma generation, ascent and eruption. Good as a systematic second pass once you have the general picture.

LEC

Volcanic processes — lecture playlist

youtube.com · playlist

A second lecture series, useful for hearing the same material explained a different way. Overlapping coverage helps more than it wastes time when the concepts are unfamiliar.

TALK

Long-form volcanology talk

youtube.com · single session

An extended single session rather than a course. Worth putting on when you want depth on one subject without committing to a full series.

Reference reading

Where to check a fact, or to see how the field frames its own open questions.

NAS
2017

Volcanic Eruptions and Their Repose, Unrest, Precursors, and Timing

nationalacademies.org · doi 10.17226/24650

A 2017 consensus report from the US National Academies. It sets out what is understood about how magma is stored and erupted, what is not, and which observations would move the field forward. If you want to know where the real research frontiers are rather than the settled textbook material, start here.

TEXT

Volcanic eruptions — CK-12 Earth Science

courses.lumenlearning.com

A plain-language chapter on eruption types, lava composition and volcanic landforms. The right level when you need to explain something to someone outside the field, or refresh a definition quickly.

Individual volcanoes

Explainers on specific systems. I will keep adding to this as I find good ones.

CASE

A volcano explained in detail

youtube.com

A case study of one volcanic system, from its structure to its eruptive history. Single-volcano explainers are often the fastest way to see how the general principles actually assemble into one real place.

Monitoring and data services

Live and archival data on volcanic activity, gas emission and surface change.

SO2

NASA MEaSUREs sulphur dioxide products

so2.gsfc.nasa.gov

Long-term satellite records of volcanic and anthropogenic SO₂ emission from NASA Goddard. Gas flux is one of the few independent checks on what a deformation signal means: ground rising with rising SO₂ points somewhere very different from ground rising in silence.

GVP

Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program

volcano.si.edu

The reference catalogue for every volcano active in the Holocene, plus a weekly activity report compiled from observatories worldwide. The volcano list behind my proximity explorer comes from here.

AVO

Alaska Volcano Observatory

avo.alaska.edu

Daily status for the Aleutian arc, written by the people who monitor it. Each volcano page gives the current colour code, what the seismic, infrasound and satellite records show, and which instruments are actually installed — useful for knowing whether a volcano is watched at all.

NOAA

NOAA NCEI Natural Hazards Viewer

ncei.noaa.gov/maps/hazards

Three linked global databases on one map: significant earthquakes back to 2150 BCE, significant eruptions back to 4360 BCE, and historical tsunamis. Events are cross-referenced, so an eruption tied to an earthquake or a tsunami is flagged and linked.

EOS

EOS LandViewer

eos.com/landviewer

Quick optical satellite imagery for any point on Earth, with band combinations you can switch in the browser. Handy for a fast look at an edifice — fresh lava, a plume, a changed crater — before committing to a radar analysis.
