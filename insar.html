Learning InSAR

Radar interferometry is not hard to start with and is very easy to get subtly wrong. These are the resources I would hand to someone beginning, in roughly the order I would hand them over.

One piece of advice before the links. Process a case you already know the answer to before you process one you do not. Pick a well-documented earthquake, make the interferogram, and check your fringes against the published result. Almost every beginner mistake — wrong reference point, unwrapping errors, atmosphere mistaken for signal — shows up immediately when there is a right answer to compare against.

EO
COL

Echoes in Space — EO College

eo-college.org

The best free introduction to radar remote sensing I know. Builds from what a radar actually measures up through interferometry and polarimetry, with the physics kept intuitive. Work through this before installing any processor and everything afterwards will make more sense.

Data and processing services

Where the radar data lives, and how to get analysis-ready products without building a processing chain first.

ASF

ASF Vertex — SAR data search

search.asf.alaska.edu

Search the Sentinel-1, NISAR and ALOS archives over any area, then use the Baseline and SBAS tools to build an interferometric stack. On-demand InSAR and terrain-corrected products can be ordered from the same interface through HyP3, at scene or burst level, without installing a processor.

COM
ET

COMET Volcanic and Magmatic Deformation Portal

comet.nerc.ac.uk

A systematic Sentinel-1 deformation survey across the world's volcanoes, with a searchable catalogue of documented deformation episodes. Check here before assuming a signal you have found is new.

My own contribution to this list. If you are working on a volcano too small for ERA5 or GACOS to help, the common-mode filtering method is open source and designed to slot into MintPy, StaMPS or LiCSBAS.
