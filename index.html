About

Measuring how the ground moves, from space.

I am a solid-Earth geodesist working at the intersection of satellite remote sensing and geophysics. My research uses Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR) to detect and interpret ground deformation of a few millimetres per year — enough to reveal unrest at volcanoes nobody is watching, to track salt rising through a desert basin, and to follow an eruption as it unfolds.

Much of that signal is buried in atmospheric noise, so I build methods to dig it out. The common-mode filtering (CMF) algorithm came out of this work: an open-source correction for small, steep volcanic edifices where global weather models are too coarse to help. I then combine the corrected geodesy with Bayesian source inversion, thermodynamic and petrological constraints, and machine learning to work back from a surface signal to the process that produced it.

My study of Taftan volcano in Iran — the first documented unrest at a volcano dormant for some 700,000 years — was covered by news outlets worldwide in October 2025.

Selected publications

Peer-reviewed research, 2021 – 2026.

Geophysical Research Letters · 2025

Spontaneous transient summit uplift at Taftan volcano, Iran

First documented unrest at a long-dormant volcano on the Makran subduction arc. Common-mode filtering resolves a 10-month, ~9 cm uplift episode sourced 490–630 m beneath the summit, with no earthquake to trigger it.

Read paper
Bulletin of Volcanology · 2026

Active magmatic processes at the Tibesti intraplate volcanoes

The first province-wide deformation baseline for a 29,000 km² volcanic province in the central Sahara. Steady subsidence at Toussidé–Yirrigué and Soborom traces back to magma reservoirs cooling and degassing since the Holocene.

Read paper
Geophysical Journal International · 2025

Surface deformation dynamics of Sierra Negra's 2018 eruption

InSAR, optical flow and pixel-offset tracking on six-day Sentinel-1 pairs recover the full eruption cycle, and a single one-day offset pair yields a magma transfer rate of roughly 60 m per hour.

Read paper
Tectonophysics · 2021

Active deformation of salt diapirs in the Kalut basin, Iran

Eight salt diapirs surveyed from 724 interferograms — one of the first InSAR studies to cover an entire diapiric basin. Stocks and walls rise at up to 8 mm/yr and spread sideways at 1–2 mm/yr.

Earthquake–Volcano Proximity Explorer

Pick a time window and a magnitude threshold, load the USGS catalogue, then click any earthquake. The tool lists every Smithsonian GVP Holocene volcano inside your chosen radius with its distance, azimuth and last known eruption, on a schematic plot and an optional satellite basemap. Built for screening which volcanoes are worth checking after a large earthquake.

Open source · peer reviewed

Common-Mode Filtering (CMF) for InSAR time series

A data-driven atmospheric correction for volcanoes too small for global weather models to resolve. It estimates the noise shared across a reference ring of pixels and removes it from the target, cutting time-series scatter by about 36% on its own and by up to 50% alongside an ERA5 correction. Designed to slot into MintPy, StaMPS and LiCSBAS workflows.

Python MintPy / StaMPS / LiCSBAS GPL-3.0
How the method works Code on GitHub DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14629139

Both tools are free to use and free to reuse. The explorer's volcano catalogue is bundled with the page and can be swapped for a newer export at any time; nothing you load is sent to a server. Proximity is spatial context only — a volcano inside the radius does not by itself imply earthquake-triggered activity.

In the news

Taftan, October 2025.

The Taftan study reached general audiences across Europe, North America and the Middle East. What travelled furthest was not the method but its consequence: a volcano widely assumed to be extinct is measurably moving, and the Makran arc has no permanent monitoring network.
